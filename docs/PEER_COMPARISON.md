# Dry-bulk peer comparison

**Dated snapshots: SBLK 9 October 2026; GNK 12 October 2026.** The same finite-life engine
is used for both companies, but each company has its own source package and assumptions.
The outputs are conditional research results rather than recommendations.

| Base-case measure | Star Bulk | Genco |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Share price | $29.70 | $26.67 |
| Asset NAV / share | $32.99 | $27.15 |
| Price / asset NAV | 0.90× | 0.98× |
| Finite-life DCF / share | $26.55 | $19.23 |
| Open-rate uplift needed to match price | 5.5% | 16.1% |
| 2027 unlevered free cash flow | $442.5m | $142.7m |

Genco screens close to its broker-anchored asset NAV, but its market price requires a larger
increase from the base freight vector in the finite-life DCF. Star Bulk has a wider base NAV
discount and a smaller implied-rate gap. Those differences are informative, but they are not
fully normalized relative value: fleet ages, class mix, charter coverage, corporate overhead,
capital returns and the evidence behind vessel marks differ.

Genco's asset evidence needs particular care. Management reported 18% net LTV using two
independent broker valuations as of 15 July 2026. The model infers the aggregate June fleet
value from that rounded ratio, allocates it across the June vessels by carrying value, and adds
Genco Volunteer at its disclosed net purchase cost. The allocation supports fleet runoff; it
does not claim that the allocated values are individual broker appraisals.

The next useful comparison step is to refresh both companies to a common reporting date and
normalize actual charter coverage, debt, dividends and drydock calendars. Until then, compare
the scenario mechanics and evidence quality alongside the headline multiples.
